# Soft Sci-Fi Honesty Taxes (Beyond Space Torpedoes)

Companion to [`rtw-space-opera-roadmap.md`](rtw-space-opera-roadmap.md).

In *Rule the Waves*, torpedoes keep the meta honest: hard to spot in time, inaccurate, and forcing a pursuer into three bad options (hold course / turn into the threat / break off). That **honesty tax** punishes overcommitment—tactical *and* design—without a rock-paper-scissors type chart.

This document does **not** aim to recreate naval torpedoes in space. It explores soft sci-fi mechanisms that can play a similar *role* (or adjacent roles): make superior ships sweat, stop “I ignore small threats” from being free, and keep chase / refusal / overbuild decisions risky.

Character drama is out of scope. Tone is soft SF: gameable abstractions, not physics lectures.

---

## What the RTW tax actually buys (the design job)

Strip the fish; keep the job description.

| Job | Why it matters |
|-----|----------------|
| **Punish pursuit** | Closing the range is not free; predator can become prey |
| **Punish design arrogance** | Glass cannons / under-screened capitals / “perfect” ships still bleed |
| **Force a real choice under uncertainty** | You act before you know if the threat is real, dense, or a bluff |
| **Stay inaccurate / unreliable** | If it always hits, it becomes a hard counter; if it never hits, it is flavor |
| **Scale with exposure** | Longer chase, tighter approach, thicker formation → more tax paid |
| **Favor the desperate / the small** | Outmatched forces get teeth without matching Displacement |

A good soft-SF honesty tax hits several of these. It does **not** need all of them. Some ideas below trade “pursuit dilemma” for “design honesty” or “map honesty” on purpose.

---

## Design axes for inventing a tax

When inventing, pick knobs deliberately:

1. **When it triggers** — chase only? any close action? after you commit FTL? when you fire first?
2. **Who can field it** — everyone? only small ships? only scavengers? only defenders?
3. **Information** — invisible until late? visible but ambiguous yield? fakeable?
4. **Response vocabulary** — what are the player’s 2–4 bad options?
5. **Failure mode** — misses, duds, friendly fire, self-harm, political cost, map cost?
6. **Counterplay without deleting it** — screens, doctrine, range, not a hard “PD beats X” erase

## Blue-sky honesty taxes

### 1. Wake debt / signature bloom

**Pitch:** Running hot to chase or force an engagement dumps a lingering **wake** that missiles, mines, or rival hunters can home on for a while after. The faster/harder you close, the brighter and longer the bloom.

**Honesty:** Pursuit writes a check cashed later—even if you win the duel. Overbuilt “always chase” doctrine paints a target on the task force.

**Bad options:** Keep sprinting (bigger wake) / cut drive and lose the prey / zig into clean space and blow the firing solution.

**Not a torpedo copy:** The tax is *your own exhaust as a liability*, not a fish in the water.

---

### 2. Lane echo / FTL graffiti

**Pitch:** Using a lane at combat speed or under arms leaves an **echo** other fleets can read: heading, rough mass, sometimes class. Ambushers and mines key off echoes. Quiet transit is slow; loud transit is fast and honest about where you were.

**Honesty:** Strategic Mobility has a visibility price. Battlecruisers that live on Dash become predictable on the beltway.

**Bad options:** Sprint and publish your vector / crawl and let them escape / break to a side lane and lose tempo.

---

### 3. Boarding weather / “soft close”

**Pitch:** Inside a certain range band, ships enter **boarding weather**—a soft-SF zone where parasite craft, drones, or telepresence cutters become far more effective, but gunnery solutions get messy for everyone. Capitals hate this band; small ships live in it.

**Honesty:** Closing to finish a wounded capital means entering their best retaliatory environment. Holding outside means they might limp away.

**Bad options:** Enter the band and risk parasites / hang back and lose the kill / break off entirely.

**Variant:** Only the *defender* gets boarding-weather bonuses (home-court gravity wells, station umbrellas)—pursuit into enemy space is extra taxed.

---

### 4. Mirror fog / fire-control contention

**Pitch:** Ships can dump **contention chaff** (soft-SF ECM fog) that degrades *everyone’s* long-range fire control in a volume—including the dumper’s. Cheap ships carry lots; expensive fleet killers carry little (tonnage went to guns/Protection).

**Honesty:** A fleeing cruiser can force the pursuer into knife-fight rules where Displacement matters less. Bluffing a dump still makes the pursuer flinch.

**Bad options:** Hold solution through the fog (eat surprises) / re-acquire after the fog (prey gains distance) / refuse the volume (prey escapes).

#### Keeping fog an honesty tax (not a trump card)

If fog just “turns off big guns while small ships keep full effect,” it becomes a hard counter: every chase ends in fog, every capital is a fool. The tax has to **hurt the dumper**, **expire**, and **fail open** often enough that calling a bluff is sometimes correct.

**Tradeoffs that preserve honesty:**

| Tradeoff | What it does | Failure if omitted |
|----------|--------------|--------------------|
| **Symmetric degradation** | Fog taxes *all* long-range fire control in the volume, including the ship that dumped it | Dumper gets a free win button |
| **Short half-life** | Fog blooms, then thins on a clock; you buy a window, not a permanent rules rewrite | Stall meta / infinite disengage |
| **Magazine / tonnage cost** | Fog generators eat mass that could have been Protection, fuel endurance, or guns; dumps are limited per fight | Spam fog every engagement |
| **Wake / bloom tell** | Dumping fog is loud on sensors—allies and third parties see it; ambush risk rises | Fog with no strategic leakage |
| **Close-range still bites both** | Inside boarding weather (element 3), fog helps less or cuts both ways harder—knife fights are messy, not “small ship paradise” | Fog + close = automatic underdog win |
| **Bluff space** | Empty dumps / thin fog / decoy canisters exist; pursuer must guess density | Fog is always real → always flinch |
| **Friendly-fire / blue-on-blue** | Linked fleet nets in fog risk hitting screens or each other | Solo dumpers only; blobs ignore the tax |
| **Immobile / slow vulnerability** | Fog does not help (or actively hurts) if you are forced into a **stationary or low-Mobility brawl**—no Dash vector to exploit the confusion | Fog boats duel line ships to death on equal terms |

**Design rule of thumb:** Fog should change *which mistake* the pursuer makes, not delete the pursuer’s ship type from the game.

#### Arsenal ships (fog-era extreme class)

One clean way to encode the tradeoff: **arsenal ships**—hulls that are mostly **weapons + engines** (Mobility + magazines), with minimal Protection and little ability to win a planted, stationary fight.

**What they are good at**

- Rapid pursuit or rapid flight while dumping fog / contention
- Threatening a pursuer who is closing fast: the chase is when their guns + fog window matter
- Forcing the RTW-shaped choice: hold course into a fogged kill pocket / break solution and lose them / refuse the volume

**What they are bad at**

- Anchored defense, blockade lines, slugfests at zero relative speed
- Surviving once fog thins if still in big-gun range without Dash left
- Trading broadsides with a Protection-heavy line ship that *refuses to chase*

**Honesty loop**

1. Prey (or escort arsenal) dumps fog and keeps Dash up → pursuer sweats.
2. Pursuer declines to close → arsenal cannot convert threat into a kill against a patient battle line.
3. Pursuer forces a stationary fight (corner them at a lane mouth, mine belt, boarding-weather cleaners) → arsenal’s missing Protection becomes the tax *they* pay.
4. Building only arsenals “wins” chases and **loses wars** that require holding ground; building only dreadnoughts wins line battles and **gets bled** by fogged pursuits.

That is the knife-edge: arsenal ships are a danger to someone **rapidly pursuing**, not a universal trump. They are the fog doctrine taken to a class extreme—same family as battlecruisers, but optimized for contention windows instead of clean gunnery.

**Optional pairings**

- With **boarding weather**: arsenal wants to threaten at the band edge while fogged; entering deep weather without screens still guts them.
- With **wake debt**: sprinting arsenal paints a later bill even if the fog chase “works.”
- With **scavengers**: dead arsenals are juicy magazines; claim tags make finishing them in fog contested.

**Prototype test:** If patient non-chasing fleets reliably farm arsenals, and reckless pursuers reliably eat arsenal fog, the tax is healthy. If arsenals also win static fights, fog has become a trump—nerf Protection-less durability in zero-Dash combat, or shorten fog windows.

---

### 5. Doom clock / magazine symmetry

**Pitch:** Ship-killer salvos are scarce and obvious when loaded into tubes—but **declaring** a salvo ready also arms a short doom clock: if you don’t fire or safe them, something bad happens (magazine risk, drive instability, treaty ping). Pursuers who stay nose-hot force the prey to either spend the salvo or eat the clock; prey who fake readiness still burn pursuer nerves.

**Honesty:** Overcommitment is psychological *and* logistical. Always-ready ship-killers aren’t free.

**Bad options:** Believe the lock and break / call the bluff and eat a real salvo / hang in the pocket until the clock resolves.

---

### 6. Sympathetic detonation / link-fire curse

**Pitch:** Modern fire-control nets let a flotilla share solutions—but a successful ship-killer or parasite hit on one linked ship can **cascade** instability through the net (soft-SF: feedback, ammo sympathetic cook-off, shield harmonic crash). Tighter doctrine nets hit harder and die together.

**Honesty:** Design overcommitment to “perfect coordinated fleet” creates a shared failure point. Loose wolf packs hit softer but don’t cascade.

**Bad options:** Stay linked for accuracy / break the net and fight dumb / open spacing and lose mutual support.

---

### 7. Claim tags / salvage magnets

**Pitch:** Scoring deep damage plants a **claim tag** visible to scavengers and rivals: this hulk will be valuable. Finishing the kill in place risks a third party arriving on the tag; towing away is slow; scuttling denies them but denies you too.

**Honesty:** Especially strong with scavenger factions. Tactical greed (chase to scrap) creates strategic leakage.

**Bad options:** Finish and linger / tag-and-leave (they rebuild it) / scuttle (no prize, no scavenger boost… today).

---

### 8. Honor fields / engagement covenants (diegetic soft rules)

**Pitch:** Some regions, gates, or ancient infrastructures impose **covenant fields**: e.g. spinal weapons fire at reduced efficiency, or ships above a mass threshold take a mobility penalty, or missiles become dumb inside the field. Locals know; invaders learn the hard way.

**Honesty:** Bringing the perfect dreadnought into the wrong volume is overcommitment. Small/weird fleets get home-court honesty taxes on big empires.

**Not universal:** If everywhere has covenants, it becomes map wallpaper. Sparse, readable fields keep it sharp.

---

### 9. Pilot leeches / crew debt

**Pitch:** Soft-SF “skill” isn’t RPG drama—it’s a **stock**. Hard pursuits, crash turns into missile spreads, and emergency boarding weather burn elite crew readiness. You can win the chase and still leave the ship clumsy for the next fight.

**Honesty:** Overusing Dash doctrine spends a resource capitals can’t buy instantly. Spam chase → brittle navy.

**Bad options:** Spend crew to hold solution / preserve crew and let them slip / rotate ships and lose concentration of force.

---

### 10. False geometry / bait mass

**Pitch:** Decoys that mimic mass/wake well enough that you can’t tell cruiser from bait until committed. Cheap to field in volume; expensive to ignore if one is real.

**Honesty:** Classic uncertainty tax. Pursuer’s options mimic RTW without needing underwater fish: commit to a ghost and get straddled by the real ship’s missiles / ignore and eat a real close pass / break off and lose both.

---

### 11. Afterimage weapons

**Pitch:** Some weapons don’t hit *now*—they pin an **afterimage** on your last hard maneuver. Missiles or lances home on where your Dash *said* you would be. Erratic evasion helps gunnery miss you but feeds afterimage seekers; straight chase helps guns but starves seekers… or vice versa depending on tuning.

**Honesty:** The response to one threat feeds the other. No clean “turn into the fish.”

**Bad options:** Smooth pursuit (guns happy, afterimages sticky) / violent jinks (seekers happy, guns blind) / disengage.

---

### 12. Parasite ecology (not full carrier era)

**Pitch:** Capitals naturally shed or attract micro-craft in combat (repair drones, sensor ticks, scavenger mites). Enemies can **seed** hostile parasites that go inert until you idle, dock, or enter boarding weather. Chase hard and you don’t clean them; linger to clean and the prey escapes.

**Honesty:** Win-the-duel-lose-the-hygiene. Design without point-defense/sanitation pays later.

---

### 13. Heat banks / thermal blackmail

**Pitch:** Soft-SF heat is a bank, not a simulation. Combat fills it; ship-killers and sprint Mobility fill it faster. At cap, you either vent (bright, vulnerable, obvious) or throttle (lose chase/guns). Prey can force you toward cap with feints.

**Honesty:** Pursuit has a thermal budget. Always-on max Dash designs hit the wall mid-opera.

---

### 14. Treaty tickers / automatic escalation

**Pitch:** Using certain weapons or closing inside a range band auto-trips **escalation ticks** visible to neutrals and rivals (soft legal/tech infrastructure, not pure politics). Finish a kill with ship-killers and you may buy a second war; let them go and you look weak *and* leave a foe.

**Honesty:** Overcommitment has map-diplomatic cost without writing a character story—just a counter and consequences.

**Use carefully:** Needs systemic diplomacy/threat, not flavor text.

---

### 15. Fold seams / risky shortcuts

**Pitch:** Prey can dive a **seam**—a soft-SF shortcut that might delete range instantly or dump both ships into a worse neighborhood (mine belt, honor field, scavenger yard approach). Pursuer chooses: follow into the seam lottery / hold the mouth / break off.

**Honesty:** Chase becomes a gamble about shared ruin. Great for beltway / frontier maps.

---

### 16. Mass taboos / displacement ceilings in combat AI

**Pitch:** Fleet automatics refuse certain closes when Displacement ratios are extreme—unless an admiral overrides (override = honesty tax: accuracy penalty, cascade risk, crew debt). Big ships literally struggle to “believe” small ships are the threat until too late.

**Honesty:** Design arrogance is partly baked into how heavy ships fight. Battlecruisers vs destroyers gets scary for the BC again.

---

### 17. Gifted ammunition / one-shot heirlooms

**Pitch:** A few ship-killers per fleet are **irreplacable** (ancient, scavenger-only, or enabler-tech prototypes). Inaccurate but terrifying. Everyone plays around whether they still have one.

**Honesty:** Bluff space like RTW torpedo turns, without needing clouds of fish. Scarcity creates the flinch.

---

### 18. Chorus guns / minimum audience

**Pitch:** Some weapons only reach full effect if enough friendly ships share the volume (resonance fire). Lone heroes underperform; packed formations overperform—and eat sympathetic cascade / wake debt harder.

**Honesty:** Overcommitment to solo capital glory fails; overcommitment to blobs fails differently. Screens matter.

---

### 19. Redline inheritance

**Pitch:** Damage isn’t just HP—it **writes redlines** into the hull (soft-SF scars). A ship that ate a ship-killer forever handles worse in boarding weather or wakes louder. Scavengers may *want* redlined hulls for unique rebuilds; conventional empires see permanent depreciation.

**Honesty:** Surviving a tax still pays installments. Future-proof ships aren’t present-proof after one bad chase.

---

### 20. The empty salvo (pure information warfare)

**Pitch:** Tubes, bays, or “kill readiness” indicators can be spoofed. The meta becomes reading tells. No physical fish required for the *flinch*; real salvos exist just often enough to punish callouts.

**Honesty:** Closest cousin to RTW’s “did they launch or fake?” without modeling underwater ballistics.

#### Empty salvos × spray-and-pray (fleeing)

Fleeing ships often cannot mount a clean firing solution aft—the geometry is wrong, the ship is shaking under Dash, sensors are looking forward toward the escape. What they *can* do is **spray and pray**: dump volume into the pursuer’s nose corridor without aiming for a surgical hit. Inaccuracy is the point; the tax is the pursuer’s uncertainty, not the expected value of each shot.

Empty salvos and spray-and-pray lock together as **information layer** + **physical layer**:

| Layer | What the fleeing ship does | What the pursuer sees |
|-------|----------------------------|------------------------|
| **Spray (real)** | Unaided aft volume fire into the chase corridor | Blooms, tracers, magazine draw, actual (low) hit chance |
| **Empty (fake)** | Spoofed readiness, fake tube doors, simulated bloom, cold launch tells | Same tells, no warheads / no beam truth |
| **Mixed** | One real spray among two fakes, or a fake then a real when the pursuer “learns” to ignore | Pattern recognition game |

**Why this is an honesty tax, not a damage engine**

- Spray hit rates while fleeing should stay **poor**—good enough to punish *ignoring* it forever, bad enough that *always flinching* loses chases.
- Empty salvos exist so the pursuer cannot solve the game with “only dodge when the math says EV is positive.”
- The fleeing ship pays too: real spray burns scarce aft magazine / heat / fog canisters; fakes burn spoof charge and may have a tell if overused; both can worsen wake debt or paint claim tags if they actually connect.

**Pursuer’s bad options (chase)**

1. **Hold course** — treat it as empty or accept the EV; risk eating a real spray (or the one real salvo in a string of fakes)
2. **Break solution / jink** — treat it as live; spray goes wide, but range opens and the prey may reach a lane mouth or boarding-weather edge on *their* terms
3. **Forced quiet** — cut Dash to read tells better; you become a better sensor platform and a worse pursuer

**Fleeing ship’s bad options**

1. **Real spray early** — might buy the flinch, but you’ve spent the teeth; later empties are less credible
2. **Empty early** — save ammo; if called, you die having never spent
3. **Spray late / mixed** — best mind game, highest skill and highest risk of mistiming into boarding weather or magazine dry

**Design honesty:** Ships with big forward batteries and no aft spray / spoof kit win clean gunnery trials and lose messy retreats. Ships that over-invest in aft spray kits become arsenal-adjacent: scary when pursued, soft when pinned in a stationary fight with empty magazines and no Dash.

#### Non-missile / non-torpedo weapons that still fit

The empty-salvo *job* is “credible aft threat + fakeable tell,” not “seeker in the water.” Soft-SF weapons that can spray, bluff, or both while fleeing:

| Weapon family | Real spray effect | Empty / bluff tell | Why it isn’t just a missile |
|---------------|-------------------|--------------------|------------------------------|
| **Aft flak / canister / grapeshot beams** | Fill a cone with cheap damage; rare lucky systems hits | Flash cartridges, dummy blooms, cold barrels with hot signatures | Area denial and nerves; terrible at range precision |
| **Dazzlers / sensor lances** | Scramble pursuer fire control for a few beats (related to mirror fog, but aimed) | Spoofed charge tone without real pump | Taxes *solutions*, not HP; overuse blinds the fleeing ship’s own aft arc too |
| **Drive-flare / torch cough** | Sudden flare that can slap a close pursuer or seed wake debt on *them* | Cosmetic flare without mass throw | Weaponized Mobility waste; hurts endurance |
| **Grav / pressure slap** | Shoves relative vectors—can break a nose-hot close or throw aim | Fake capacitor surge | Soft-SF “shove,” not a warhead; weak if pursuer already matched vectors |
| **Spine overcharge dump** | Spinal/beam ships vent a dirty aft discharge when they can’t traverse the mount forward in time | Charge spoof without release | Turns capital main guns into desperate chase taxes; risks redlining own spine |
| **Minelet crumbs / wake tacks** | Seed slow hazards behind you (closer to mines than torpedoes) | Empty racks with loud doors | Punishes *holding the exact chase line*; patient pursuers cut corners and ignore |
| **Parasite kick / boarding seeds** | Toss inert ticks that arm if the pursuer enters boarding weather | Empty pods | Links to element 3: the spray’s real teeth show up only if they keep closing |
| **Chorus scrap fire** | Secondaries from several fleeing ships share a “wall” of nuisance fire | One ship spoofs the chorus handshake | Needs friends; solo empty chorus is a tell |
| **Afterimage pins** (element 11) | Tag the pursuer’s predicted Dash line | Fake pin with no seeker follow-up | The “salvo” is a prediction marker, not a fish |

**Patterns that work especially well**

1. **Flak + empty bloom** — most readable RTW cousin without seekers: cone of junk, fakeable muzzle show, poor accuracy while fleeing.
2. **Dazzler + empty charge** — non-lethal primary effect; pursuer flinches because losing fire control mid-close is how predators become prey in boarding weather.
3. **Parasite seeds + empty pods** — spray looks weak *now*; becomes brutal only if the pursuer insists on finishing inside boarding weather. Empty pods train them to ignore—until one pod is real.
4. **Spine dump + spoof** — gives battleships / battlecruisers a retreat tax without turning them into missile boats; pairs with Protection lock (you cannot “refit” a spine into a safe chase weapon without rebuild-scale work).

**What to avoid**

- Making aft spray accurate enough that fleeing is the best offense → honesty tax becomes kiting meta.
- Making empties free and perfect → pursuer always flinches → mobility always wins.
- Hard “PD deletes all spray” → empties stop mattering because real spray never threatens.

**Prototype test:** In a chase sandbox, a player who *always* holds course should sometimes die to real spray; a player who *always* jinks should rarely catch the prey; a player who reads mixed empty/real patterns should outperform both—without needing missile ballistics.

## Terrain-changing escapes

Fleeing by **stirring the local void**—not just outrunning on calm space. Soft-SF space is already weatherful: gravity knots, temporal eddies, phantom tides, ghost reefs, lane moods, boarding weather. Escape tools **wake or twist** that weather so the pursuer inherits a worse sky.

These are powerful because they attack the pursuer’s *plan*, not their HP. That is also why they become trump cards if untuned: every peer fight opens with “curdle the cosmos until I win.”

### Design job

| Wanted | Unwanted |
|--------|----------|
| A chased ship can buy distance, uncertainty, or a bad choice for the pursuer | A peer duelist snaps their fingers and deletes the enemy’s Mobility/Protection advantages |
| Weather you raise is a **spend**, and the sky stays weird for whoever comes next | Weather is free, permanent, and obedient only to you |
| Useful in retreat *and* sometimes in attack—but attack uses a different risk profile | Only cowards benefit, or only attackers benefit |

### Escape tools (sky-stirrers)

Lean into soft-SF phenomena. No need to justify mass budgets or vacuum physics—only readable effects and leftovers.

| Tool | What it does while fleeing | Leftover sky |
|------|----------------------------|--------------|
| **Gravity knot / well yank** | Pinch a local tug so the pursuer’s Dash line bends, stalls, or overshoots | A lingering heavy spot; later traffic feels “downhill” toward it |
| **Temporal eddy** | Slip the chase clock—seconds stretch or stutter in a pocket so solutions rot and ranges lie | Eddy fades slow; clocks and fire-control disagree inside it for a while |
| **Phantom tide** | A soft current that carries light ships one way and fights heavy keels the other | Tide direction may reverse; you might fight your own current coming back |
| **Contention fog / mirror weather** | Sensor-soup bloom (element 4); long eyes go blind | Fog hangs; anyone entering fights by rumor |
| **Boarding-weather seed** | Thickens the close band behind you (element 3) | A sticky knife-fight climate on the map |
| **Ghost reef** | Summon or unmask a hazard shoal (debris, crystal, bone-yard—pick a flavor) | Reef stays; scavengers love it; Dash through it is rude |
| **Lane mood / gate sulk** | Offend a lane mouth so it sulks—transitions get ugly unless you brake and coax | Mood may remember *you* as the offender next transit |
| **Fold seam crack** | Tear a shortcut lottery (element 15) | Seam stays hungry; third parties can dive it |
| **Covenant wake** | Trip an honor-field / ancient rule-weather (element 8) | Covenant may not love your Displacement next visit |
| **Claim-tag flare** | Make the volume “interesting” to scavengers/neutrals (element 7) | Social weather: company arrives |

Bluffs still apply: **empty eddies**, fake gravity tells, spoofed reef blooms—same information game as empty salvos, painted on the sky.

### Keeping them from trumping peer battles

Peer battle = similar Displacement, similar doctrine, neither side is clearly prey yet. Sky-stirring must feel **natural in a chase** and **awkward in a fair fight**.

**1. Chase-shaped weather**  
Many stirrers only bloom when the sky already has a **hunt in it**—a clear pursuer, a fleeing wake, a stretched chase corridor. In a peer stare-down or slow circling duel, the same tool is thin, brief, or sulks and refuses. Fleeing wakes the void; mutual posing does not.

**2. Weather has no friends**  
What you raise is **not loyal**. Gravity knots tug both fleets; temporal eddies rot everyone’s solutions; boarding weather feeds whoever brought cutters; phantom tides flip. In a peer fight, stirring often helps the side that wanted chaos—which may be your opponent.

**3. Rare reagents, not cargo math**  
Stirrers spend scarce soft-SF stocks: bottled eddies, well-keys, tide charms, fog mirrors, seam sparks. Peer battles that open with mutual sky-spam **dry both sides** into a naked gunnery fight—the weather witch is then behind. Chase use: one good spend. Peer use: attrition trap.

**4. Readable wind-up**  
The void shows tells before it breaks—shimmer, clock-skew, well-groan. In a chase, the pursuer is already committed and may not wait. In a peer fight, the opponent can punish the ritual with a spine volley or simply hold outside until the bloom dies.

**5. Wrong weather for your hull**  
Dreadnoughts raising boarding weather water a garden for destroyers. Light ships yanking mass covenants may strand their own allied capital. Peer use wants coordination; solo panic stirring is a chase tool.

**6. Scarred sky**  
Repeated stirring in one region leaves **map weather scars**: sulky lanes, scavenger weather, clocks that never sit still. Peer empires that spam editors win the battle and lose the beltway. Chase use is local and rare; peer spam is strategically stupid.

**7. Fail-open and bluff**  
Fake eddy / empty well (tie to empty salvos). In peer battle, bluffing weather to force a flinch is weaker—neither side must hold a chase line; they can drift until the tell resolves. In a chase, drifting away *is* escape. Same tool, different EV.

**8. Anchor / entanglement**  
If you stay to exploit the sky you made (peer “I raise an eddy then knife you”), you pay an **anchor tax**: the weather sticks to *you*—slower Dash, boarding climate clinging to your hull, claim-light on your wake. Fleeing *through* your own storm and not lingering keeps it an escape tool.

**Rule of thumb:** In a peer fight, sky-stirring should feel like **betting the leftover weather**. In a chase, it should feel like **selling the pursuer a bad sky**.

### Still useful (not nerfed into flavor)

Do not solve trumps by making stirrers useless. Keep clear wins for correct use:

- Prey with one eddy/fog/well spend against a nose-hot BC should sometimes escape or flip.
- A peer that *forces* a chase (overcommit Dash) should get taxed by the same tools.
- A prepared ghost reef or sulking gate at a lane mouth should matter—as **positional** weather with setup cost, not an instant mid-duel button.
- Scavenger / underdog factions can specialize in sky-stirring without also winning clean peer line battles.

**Health check:** Peer line-battle win rate barely moves when both sides have stirrers; chase escape rate and “punish overchase” rate move a lot.

### Pursuer boarding: stirring the sky on the attack

Boarding weather is usually prey-flavored. Pursuers can hold stirrers too—and that turns the void into a herding tool.

| Pursuer stir | Intent | Risk (keeps it honest) |
|--------------|--------|------------------------|
| **Seed boarding weather ahead** | Force them through a knife climate, or brake into guns | You must contest the same climate to finish; screens eat the tax if you’re Protection-light |
| **Drop a temporal eddy on their exit** | Rot their escape solutions and empty-salvo tells | Your own finish timer lies too; arsenal pursuers cope, patient dreadnoughts seethe |
| **Yank a gravity knot under their keel** | Bend their line toward a wall, reef, or your boarders | Knot stays; if they slip, *you* fall downhill into your own well on the follow-through |
| **Fog the lane mouth** | Blind the clean exit | Long-gun finish dies with it |
| **Raise a ghost reef ahead** | Herd them off friendly vectors | Reef remains as scavenger country |
| **Sulking gate behind you** | Cut their reinforcements once you’re in | You maroon your own follow-on forces |
| **Claim-tag flare mid-chase** | Invite company that favors you | Company does not stay loyal |
| **Covenant wake vs their pet weapon** | e.g. damp spinal romance if they’re a spine boat | Covenant may damp *your* boarding gear too—read the omen |

**Boarding-specific loop**

1. Pursuer thickens boarding weather / drops an eddy on the prey’s line → clean flight and long-gun stories weaken.
2. Prey must dive the weird sky (parasites, clock-skew, crew strain) or break the escape vector (caught).
3. Pursuer who enters to board inherits their own weather: blind eyes, heavy keel, sticky climate, scavenger ticks.
4. A successful board inside self-made weather should feel **expensive and decisive**—not a free convert of every chase into a capture.

**Arsenal pursuers** vs **line pursuers**

- Arsenals *want* fog, eddies, and boarding climate—messy close, then board or magazine-dump.
- Line ships want to **threaten** weather without bathing in it—herd with a well or reef, finish with guns at the bright edge.
- If arsenals can board through self-made storms with no entanglement, sky-stirring becomes a trump again; keep anchor/crew taxes on the boarder.

**Empty sky** (bluff stirrers) in pursuer hands: fake eddy to shove prey into a worse lane; if they call and hold course, nothing happens and you’ve burned tempo. Same mind game as empty salvos, written on the weather.

## Evaluation checklist

For each candidate, ask:

1. Does a **superior** ship still sweat?
2. Can a **weaker** force force a decision (not necessarily win)?
3. Are there **2–4 responses**, each clearly bad in a different way?
4. Is there **uncertainty** (bluff, inaccuracy, delay), not a guaranteed delete?
5. Does it punish **design** overcommitment, **tactical** overcommitment, or both?
6. Can conventional PD/screens **mitigate** without erasing?
7. Does it stay soft-SF readable in UI (one or two meters/tells)?
8. Does it accidentally create a hard counter loop?

If (1)–(4) are strong, it can carry an era even if it barely resembles a torpedo.

## Recommendation for prototyping

Don’t start by picking the coolest fiction. Start by picking the **job**:

- Need RTW-like chase dilemmas first → prototype **empty salvo + boarding weather** or **wake debt + false geometry**.
- Need map-layer honesty first → prototype **lane echo + claim tags**.
- Need scavenger synergy first → prototype **claim tags + redline inheritance**.
- Need flee-without-trump tools → prototype **sky-stirrers** (eddies, gravity knots, fog) with chase-shaped weather + disloyal leftovers + anchor tax; then try pursuer herding into boarding weather.

Prove the flinch in a tiny battle/map sandbox before decorating with lore.
